High-Q Photonic Crystal Nanobeam Cavities for On-Chip Sensing

A. Researcher1, B. Colleague2

Abstract. We report the design, fabrication and characterization of one-dimensional photonic crystal nanobeam cavities in silicon-on-insulator, reaching a measured loaded quality factor of 1.2 × 106 at a resonant wavelength near 1550 nm. The devices exhibit a mode volume of 0.68 (λ/n)3, enabling strong light–matter interaction in a footprint compatible with dense photonic integration [1], [2].

1. Introduction

Optical microcavities confine light to small volumes for long times, and the ratio of these two quantities governs the strength of light–matter coupling. Over the past two decades, photonic crystal cavities have emerged as the platform of choice for reaching simultaneously high quality factors and wavelength-scale mode volumes [3].

In this work we focus on the nanobeam geometry, in which a one-dimensional lattice of holes is etched into a suspended waveguide. Compared with two-dimensional slab cavities, nanobeams offer a smaller footprint, simpler fabrication, and natural compatibility with bus-waveguide coupling.

2. Design and Simulation

The cavity was designed using three-dimensional finite-difference time-domain simulations. A quadratic taper of the hole radius over twelve periods produces a Gaussian-like field envelope, which suppresses out-of-plane radiation losses.

3. Results

  • Devices were fabricated on a 220 nm silicon-on-insulator wafer using electron-beam lithography.
  • The measured linewidth of 1.3 pm corresponds to a loaded quality factor of 1.2 million.
  • Thermal tuning of 0.08 nm/K was observed, consistent with the thermo-optic coefficient of silicon.

We have demonstrated nanobeam cavities with record quality factors in a CMOS-compatible platform. Future work will integrate microfluidic channels for label-free biosensing.
